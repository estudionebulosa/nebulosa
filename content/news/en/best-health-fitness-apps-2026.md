Six weeks ago we set ourselves one concrete question: **which health apps
deserve a spot on your home screen in 2026**. We tested 14 apps on a Pixel and
an iPhone, with a fixed methodology: one week to adjust, four of real use and
one week of no supporting installs.

## How we evaluated

Every app was scored on five criteria, with the same recipe for all of them:

| Criterion | Weight | What we looked at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real usefulness | 35% | Is it used without remembering that it should be? |
| Design & accessibility | 25% | Contrast, touch targets, reduced motion |
| Privacy | 20% | Mandatory account, ads, third parties |
| Works offline | 10% | Logging and browsing without network |
| Cost | 10% | Fair price for what it delivers |

> "A health app fails when the user abandons it in the first week; that is why
> we reward habit over feature count."

## Pulse: the most complete one

Pulse builds plans that adapt to what you actually did last week. Miss two
sessions and it reorders instead of punishing you, and the whole log works
**offline** — a detail none of the other four apps handles as well.

Its strengths:

- Adaptive plans that change with your real performance.
- Full offline logging with encrypted sync.
- No mandatory account: the account is optional.

## Verso, for the habit

If your goal is not the gym but reading consistency, Verso is the pick: a
reading journal with habit tracking, quotes and encrypted sync. Its limit is
that it does not integrate with third-party readers.

## Where we'd drop the ball: meditation and sleep

Meditation apps only stand out if the sessions fit a real break (three to
five minutes). For sleep, the least accurate app was the one trying to guess
sleep from the phone alone: **using smartwatch data changes accuracy
completely**.

## Conclusion

If you install only one, install **Pulse** for training and **Verso** if you
want to read more. Both pass the criterion we care about most: they still
work in the week you decided not to use them.
